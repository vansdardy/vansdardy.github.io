## Problem Statement

Let us first begin with the original problem statement, and then figure out how to interpret the situation.

You are on a game show where you are presented with three doors to choose from. The host of the show tells you that behind each door is either your dream car or a goat. Behind two of these doors will be two goats, one each; behind ***only*** one of these doors will be your dream car.
The host asks you to make a choice between the three doors. If you chose the door behind which is your dream car, you get the car. After you have locked in a choice, the host tells you, "Before you chose, I actually already know what is behind each of the doors". The host continues, "I am actually going to help you a little bit here." The host then opens one of the two doors which you did not choose and revealed a goat.
Now, the host asks you, "See, I have helped you eliminate an option. You have another chance here, do you keep your original choice, or do you switch your choice to the other door?"

## Textbook Answer and Explanation

This is one of most classic problems you would always encounter in an undergraduate mathematics course teaching about probability theory, specifically, on the topic of ***conditional probability*** and ***Bayes' Theorem***.
